PROJECT PURPOSE

Build a wheelchair-mounted obstacle-awareness prototype using:
- One ToF distance sensor.
- One MPU6050 IMU.
- Two SG90 servos.
- One ESP32-WROOM development board.
- One 1.3-inch OLED.

The ToF sensor sits on a two-servo pan/tilt bracket:
- Pan servo scans left, centre and right.
- Tilt servo compensates for wheelchair pitch using the frame-mounted MPU6050.

The OLED displays directional obstacle information and STOP warnings.

IMPORTANT:

We have no wheelchair motor driver, brake interface or certified safety controller.
Do not implement or claim automatic wheelchair stopping.
Do not connect this prototype to the wheelchair propulsion electronics.

MECHANICAL DESIGN

Mount the MPU6050 rigidly on the wheelchair frame or test base, not on the moving ToF platform.

Mount the ToF sensor on the pan/tilt bracket.

Servo responsibilities:
1. Pan: left/centre/right scanning relative to the chair.
2. Tilt: approximate pitch compensation.

Clearly explain:
- Two servos used for pan and tilt do not provide independent roll stabilization.
- MPU6050 alone does not provide drift-free absolute yaw.
- This arrangement is approximate pitch compensation, not guaranteed stabilization.
- Sensor measurements are sequential, not simultaneous 360-degree coverage.
- Pan/tilt geometry can couple the axes; do not claim simple pitch subtraction is accurate at every pan angle.

Start with small base tilts and modest scan angles.
If necessary, restrict the stabilization demonstration to centre-facing measurements until off-centre performance has been tested.

Prevent:
- Cable twisting.
- Mechanical collisions.
- Servo hard-stop contact.
- Pinch points.
- Excessive bracket loading.

Choose conservative adjustable angle limits, not automatic 0–180-degree sweeps.

POWER AND ELECTRICAL REQUIREMENTS

An ESP32 development board may accept 5 V through USB or its appropriate labelled 5V/VIN input, depending on the board.
The ESP32 chip and GPIO operate at 3.3 V logic.

Require the exact board details before confirming power wiring.

Servos:
- Power both SG90 servos directly from a suitably rated regulated 5 V supply branch.
- Never power them from the ESP32 3V3 pin.
- Avoid routing servo load current through the development board.
- Connect servo-supply ground and ESP32 ground.
- Explain that a separate power branch does not necessarily require a separate battery.
- Confirm supply capability for simultaneous servo movement and transient loads.
- Suggest a suitable bulk capacitor near the servo rail if available.
- Do not claim a capacitor fixes an undersized supply.
- Avoid simultaneous USB and external VIN power unless the board’s power arrangement is confirmed.

Sensors/display:
- Verify accepted voltage and logic levels for each exact breakout.
- Do not assume every breakout accepts 5 V.
- Ensure I2C pull-ups do not pull ESP32 SDA/SCL to 5 V.
- Do not put 5 V onto ESP32 GPIO.

Do not use an XL6009 or any other power converter unless separately justified and its output is measured before connection.

PROVISIONAL PIN PLAN

For a confirmed standard ESP32-WROOM development board:

I2C:
- GPIO 21: SDA.
- GPIO 22: SCL.

Servo signals:
- GPIO 18: pan servo.
- GPIO 19: tilt servo.

All confirmed I2C modules may share the bus if their addresses do not conflict.

Typical addresses to check, not assume:
- MPU6050: 0x68 or 0x69.
- OLED: 0x3C or 0x3D.
- Many VL53-series sensors: 0x29.

If the OLED is SPI, revise the pin plan explicitly.
Avoid boot-strapping, flash-connected and input-only pins for servo outputs.

IMU CALIBRATION AND FILTERING

At startup:
1. Initialize MPU6050.
2. Ask the user to hold the base still in the chosen reference position.
3. Collect calibration samples.
4. Reject/retry calibration if substantial movement is detected.
5. Record reference offsets.

Use a simple documented complementary filter:
- Integrate gyro rates.
- Correct pitch/roll estimate using accelerometer-derived orientation.
- Use measured elapsed time.
- Handle unreasonable time gaps.
- Apply the correct unit conversions for the selected library.

Explain that acceleration and impacts can distort accelerometer-based tilt.
Do not convert every vibration into a precise terrain or safety claim.

Tilt control:
- Configurable neutral angle.
- Configurable compensation direction/sign.
- Configurable gain.
- Small deadband.
- Angle limits.
- Slew-rate limit.
- Filtered commands to reduce chatter.

Mark the sign convention clearly.
Provide a slow bench test to determine whether compensation reverses or amplifies base pitch.
Do not enable unrestricted stabilization before that test.

SCANNING STATE MACHINE

Use cooperative non-blocking scheduling with millis()/micros().
Avoid long delay() calls in normal operation.

Suggested states:
1. COMMAND_PAN.
2. WAIT_FOR_SETTLE.
3. START_RANGE.
4. WAIT_FOR_RANGE.
5. RECORD_RESULT.
6. ADVANCE_SECTOR.

Suggested sequence:
CENTRE → LEFT → CENTRE → RIGHT → repeat.

Angles must be configurable and calibrated to the actual bracket.
Use modest offsets from centre initially.

Servo position is commanded, not measured.
SG90 does not provide position feedback to this firmware.
Describe settling time as an empirical assumption.

Only record a sector reading after:
- Pan movement has had time to settle.
- Tilt command is sufficiently stable.
- ToF acquisition has completed successfully.

If tilt moves substantially during acquisition:
- Discard/mark the reading uncertain, or restart measurement.
- Prevent endless restarts with a bounded timeout.
- Mark the sector unknown if stable acquisition cannot be obtained.

Provide configurable:
- Scan angles.
- Servo movement rate.
- Settling time.
- ToF timing budget where supported.
- Sample count.
- Maximum acquisition timeout.
- Maximum data age.

Check scan-cycle timing against freshness settings.
Do not set freshness shorter than the achievable scan revisit interval.

DISTANCE FILTERING AND WARNINGS

Use sensor-specific valid-range/status checks.
Reject invalid readings before filtering.

Use a small median filter or another simple documented robust filter.

Do not let filtering hide an immediately valid close obstacle.
A fresh valid measurement inside the configured STOP range should raise a warning promptly.
Use confirmation/hysteresis primarily for clearing warnings.

Demo-only initial configurable thresholds:
- STOP distance: 600 mm.
- CAUTION distance: 1200 mm.

These are adjustable demonstration values, not validated wheelchair braking distances.

Threshold rules:
- Any fresh valid sector below STOP distance:
  STOP_WARNING with the corresponding direction.
- Any fresh valid sector below CAUTION distance:
  OBSTACLE_WARNING.
- Missing, invalid or stale required sector:
  Unknown/degraded state; never overall clear.
- All required sectors fresh and valid above warning thresholds:
  NO NEAR OBSTACLE.

Maintain known obstacle warnings even if another sector becomes invalid.
For example:
“STOP LEFT / RIGHT UNKNOWN.”

Use configurable hysteresis and clearing requirements to prevent flicker.
Do not erase an obstacle warning just because the next read timed out.

FAILURE HANDLING

Handle:
- ToF initialization failure.
- MPU6050 initialization failure.
- OLED initialization failure.
- I2C timeout/bus error.
- Out-of-range ToF results.
- Stale readings.
- Failed IMU calibration.
- Servo compensation saturation.
- Excessive motion preventing stable reads.

Use bounded library timeouts where supported.
Do not busy-wait forever for a sensor.

If IMU data fails:
- Disable compensation.
- Move toward a conservative neutral position only if mechanically appropriate.
- Show IMU ERROR / STABILIZATION OFF.
- Do not claim stabilized measurements.

If ToF data fails:
- Do not show clear.
- Show DISTANCE UNKNOWN / STOP ADVISORY.

If OLED fails:
- Report the failure through Serial.
- Treat the build as unsuitable for the intended user-facing demo until fixed.

Do not claim the firmware can detect servo stall or power failure without relevant feedback hardware.
